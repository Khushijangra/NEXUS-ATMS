# VII. Discussion

## 7.1 Feature Representation Analysis
The forensic evaluation of the offline feature extraction pipeline confirms the robust nature of the dense visual embeddings extracted via the VideoMAE architecture. The extracted 768-dimensional representations exhibit a measured feature entropy of $0.7638$, indicating a rich embedding space capable of encoding complex spatiotemporal dynamics. Furthermore, the extreme sparsity metric ($3.75 \times 10^{-8}$) suggests that the encoded feature vectors are highly dense, avoiding trivial sparse representations. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) reveals a well-distributed spectrum of eigenvalues, with the primary components ($\lambda_1 = 6.068$, $\lambda_2 = 4.775$, $\lambda_3 = 3.708$) demonstrating that variance is efficiently captured across multiple orthogonal dimensions. This representation richness confirms that the latent space contains significant informational capacity; however, such high dimensionality inherently poses computational and representational challenges for downstream reinforcement learning control policies.

## 7.2 Anomaly Modeling Analysis
The integration of Multivariate Unsupervised Latent Density Estimation (MULDE) provides a highly stable scalar representation of traffic scene abnormalities. Analysis of the anomaly score distribution across the 100 evaluation sequences indicates a tightly bounded operational envelope, with a mean score of $14.10$, ranging strictly between $14.07$ and $14.18$. The intra-sequence variance is exceptionally low, confined between $0.00003$ and $0.00015$. This tightly constrained behavior demonstrates the robustness of the anomaly modeling framework. Importantly, the offline deterministic evaluation yielded zero $L_1$ and $L_2$ drifts across 1,000 iterations, alongside a flawless cosine similarity of $1.0$ and zero standard deviation, guaranteeing strict temporal consistency. Consequently, the anomaly scalar provides an interpretable and mathematically stable proxy for incident detection that is resilient to stochastic noise.

## 7.3 Reinforcement Learning Analysis
The empirical outcomes from the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) ablation study reveal critical insights into state-space representation for intelligent traffic control. The Baseline architecture, relying solely on structured telemetry from the microscopic traffic simulator, achieved a mean episodic reward of $-0.875$ ($SD=0.301$). The introduction of the compact anomaly scalar (Anomaly mode) slightly improved mean performance to $-0.839$, albeit with an increased variance ($SD=0.785$). This suggests that scalar contextual signals provide complementary information without overwhelming the policy network.

Conversely, the direct concatenation of the 768-dimensional feature embeddings drastically degraded performance. The Feature mode yielded a lower mean reward of $-1.057$ ($SD=1.105$), and the Full mode (combining features and anomaly scores) further exacerbated instability, dropping the mean reward to $-1.381$ ($SD=1.445$). This deterioration is symptomatic of a state-space explosion; the injection of dense, high-dimensional visual embeddings into a shallow PPO network drastically increases sample complexity. Under a constrained short training horizon, the policy optimization process becomes highly unstable as the network attempts to map spurious visual correlations to discrete traffic control actions.

## 7.4 Runtime and Deployment Analysis
A prerequisite for cyber-physical transportation systems is real-time operational feasibility. The proposed dual-stream bottleneck architecture comfortably satisfies these requirements. Hardware profiling demonstrates an effective throughput of $47.16$ frames per second (FPS), comfortably exceeding the standard 30 FPS threshold for live video monitoring. Following an initial cold-start latency of $306.02$ ms, the system achieves a warm median latency of $21.59$ ms. Tail latencies remain strictly bounded, with $P_{95}$ and $P_{99}$ metrics recorded at $25.91$ ms and $36.77$ ms, respectively. Furthermore, the memory footprint is highly optimized for edge deployment; the inference engine necessitates only $1885.12$ MB of system RAM and a nominal $11.65$ MB of VRAM, corresponding to a modest $31\%$ GPU utilization on entry-level hardware. These constraints validate the cyber-physical deployment feasibility of the framework on constrained edge compute nodes.

## 7.5 Statistical Significance Analysis
Equivalence testing provides a rigorous interpretation of the observed architectural performances. Non-parametric analyses comparing the Anomaly mode to the Baseline yielded a $p$-value of $0.813$, a Cohen's $d$ of $0.060$, and a Cliff's $\delta$ of $0.20$, confirming a negligible but non-detrimental effect size. Comparisons involving the high-dimensional embeddings—specifically the Feature ($p=1.000$, Cohen's $d=-0.225$) and Full architectures ($p=0.625$, Cohen's $d=-0.486$)—exhibited small negative effect sizes. While the mean rewards visually demonstrate performance degradation, the high variance prevents the assertion of strong statistical significance against the baseline. The primary practical significance of these findings lies in highlighting the limitations of raw embedding concatenation without intermediate semantic compression.

## 7.6 Scientific Contributions
The empirical evaluation establishes the following scientific contributions. First, the architecture successfully demonstrates that a perception pipeline leveraging self-supervised video transformers and latent density estimation can operate deterministically at high framerates on edge-constrained hardware. Second, the reinforcement learning ablation study concretely establishes that high-dimensional visual embeddings destabilize short-horizon RL, whereas compact anomaly representations preserve policy robustness. The framework validates the necessity of representational bottlenecks when bridging heavy visual perception models with adaptive traffic control algorithms.

## 7.7 Limitations
While mathematically and empirically robust, this study possesses several limitations. The execution relied on offline feature caching rather than online, real-time VideoMAE execution, largely due to computational constraints during continuous RL training. Furthermore, the reinforcement learning policies were evaluated over a limited training horizon of 1,000 timesteps within a single-intersection simulation environment; this constrained scope restricted the ability of the neural policies to fully converge when subjected to expansive 768-dimensional state spaces. The dataset limitations inherent in static offline extraction also prohibit closed-loop visual feedback where agent actions directly alter the perceived visual scene.

## 7.8 Future Research Directions
Future research must prioritize techniques for semantic compression and dimensionality reduction to bridge the gap between rich perception and robust control. Methods such as latent state compression and the implementation of transformer-based policies may better process high-dimensional embeddings over longer horizons. Additionally, extending the simulation to multi-agent traffic control environments utilizing graph reinforcement learning will be essential for network-wide coordination. Finally, integrating online anomaly adaptation mechanisms that continuously update latent density models in response to dynamic environmental shifts remains a critical trajectory for future intelligent transportation systems.

# VIII. Conclusion

This research presented and rigorously validated a dual-stream architecture combining self-supervised video perception, unsupervised latent density estimation, and reinforcement learning for intelligent traffic signal control. The mathematical framework leveraged VideoMAE embeddings and MULDE scoring to quantify scene abnormalities, which were subsequently integrated as contextual observations for a Proximal Policy Optimization agent. Through strict forensic evaluation and an exhaustive ablation study across multiple stochastic seeds, the experimental validation confirmed the structural determinism and operational feasibility of the pipeline.

The principal finding of this study dictates that the direct injection of high-dimensional visual embeddings into RL policies degrades learning stability. In contrast, a secondary finding establishes that compact anomaly representations preserve policy robustness while providing essential contextual information. Computationally, the perception pipeline achieves real-time execution at 47.16 FPS with modest hardware requirements, notably utilizing just 11.65 MB of VRAM.

Ultimately, the results underscore the critical importance of semantic compression. The challenges of multimodal RL necessitate thoughtful architectural bottlenecks to prevent state-space explosion, ensuring scalable and adaptive deployments in future intelligent transportation systems.
